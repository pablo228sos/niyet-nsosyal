# Problem evidence notes

This file keeps the problem statement grounded. We should not build the report around one app-store review.

## What we can support academically

A useful piece of evidence comes from Joyce and Kraut's study of 2,777 newcomers across six public newsgroups. About 61% of first posts received a reply. Newcomers who received a reply were more likely to post again. In the paper's model, receiving a reply increased the probability of posting again by about 12 percentage points.

This does not prove that the same effect size applies to NSosyal in 2026. The study is older and the platforms are different. We can use it to support the narrower statement that receiving a response has been linked to continued participation in online communities.

Source:
- Joyce, E., Kraut, R. E. Predicting Continued Participation in Newsgroups. Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication, 2006, 11(3), 723-747.
- https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/3/723/4617705

## What we can support about recommendation systems

Popularity bias is a documented recommender-system problem. Research reviews show that recommendation systems can concentrate exposure on already popular items and reduce long-tail visibility.

This supports testing whether an interaction-routing layer can spread useful response opportunities more evenly. It does not prove that NSosyal currently has a measured popularity-bias problem.

Source:
- Klimashevskaia, A., Jannach, D., Elahi, M., Trattner, C. A survey on popularity bias in recommender systems, 2024.
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11257-024-09406-0

## What we can support about NSosyal

Public statements about NSosyal say the platform prioritizes real interaction, avoids ad-driven profiling and uses non-manipulative feed design. That makes useful human interaction a product-aligned outcome for this project.

Public product announcements also show that NSosyal already has normal social features and active product development. We should present NIYET as a layer that could fit into the platform, not as a claim that NSosyal lacks basic social functions.

Sources:

- https://www.aa.com.tr/en/turkiye/new-features-added-to-turkish-social-media-platform-nsosyal/3780881

## App-store and complaint evidence

User reviews can be used as qualitative examples of pain points, but not as population statistics unless we collect them systematically.

If we include reviews in the report we should state:
- where they came from
- the date range
- how many reviews we inspected
- how we grouped complaints
- that review platforms are self-selected samples

A single quote should never carry the main problem statement.

## Problem statement we can defend

A social feed can be good at deciding what content a user is likely to consume while still having no explicit mechanism for allocating limited willing human attention to posts that need a response. This creates a different optimization problem for help, feedback and collaboration requests, especially when many requests compete for a small set of capable responders.

NIYET tests whether treating those requests as a capacity-constrained interaction allocation problem can improve coverage without overloading the same responders.

## Claims to avoid

Do not write:
- "most NSosyal users cannot get replies"
- "NSosyal has a retention problem caused by unanswered posts"
- "NIYET will increase retention by 12%"
- "popularity bias is proven on NSosyal"

We do not currently have data to support those claims.
